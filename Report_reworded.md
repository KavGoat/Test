## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Background

The Australian Antarctic Division (AAD) manages asbestos-containing materials (ACMs) at its Antarctic and sub-Antarctic stations through the Asbestos Management Plan (AMP) and station Asbestos Registers. These documents identify, assess and manage ACMs, but they do not set out when asbestos should be removed, how removal should be prioritised, or what the cost and program implications would be.

This Asbestos Removal Program (ARP) fills that gap. It sets a consistent method for assessing, prioritising, scheduling and costing asbestos removal across AAD stations over a 10-year planning horizon. It sits alongside the AMP and Asbestos Registers rather than replacing them, and deals with planning and cost rather than how individual removal jobs are carried out.

### 1.2 Scope

The ARP covers the ACMs listed in the current Asbestos Registers for Casey, Davis, Macquarie Island and Mawson stations. It includes a prioritisation method, planning assumptions, indicative removal and reinstatement scope, scheduling, constraints and high-level cost forecasts to support long-term asset and maintenance planning.

Detailed removal methods, removal control plans, regulatory notifications, air monitoring, decontamination, waste disposal, clearance inspections and other execution requirements are outside the scope of the ARP. These are to be developed for each removal work package before work starts.

## 2 Existing Management

### 2.1 Compliance

AAD developed the AMP in line with the national Work Health and Safety (WHS) Regulations and the asbestos Codes of Practice. The ARP uses the AMP as its primary reference and is consistent with its policies.

### 2.2 Asbestos Management Plan

The AMP for the Australian Antarctic Territory and Macquarie Island is the main tool for managing ACM. It sets out how to protect the health and safety of employees and expeditioners once ACM has been identified.

The ARP does not change or override the AMP. It focuses on prioritising and scheduling AAD's removal decisions and refers back to the AMP where needed. Where removal is not practicable or preferred, ACM can be protected, enclosed, sealed, encapsulated or repaired as directed by the AMP or noted in the Asbestos Register.

### 2.3 Asbestos Registers

The following Asbestos Registers were provided for review and used in the tool:

- Asbestos Register – Casey Station (Version 7, 25/06/2026)
- Asbestos Register – Davis Station (Version 7, 04/06/2026)
- Asbestos Register – Macquarie Island Station (Version 7, 09/06/2026)
- Asbestos Register – Mawson Station (Version 7, 09/06/2026)

### 2.4 ARP Interface with Existing Registers

The AMP remains the governing document for identifying, assessing and managing ACM, and the Asbestos Registers remain the source of information on ACM location, condition, inspections and management actions. The ARP draws on both to prioritise, schedule and cost future removal, and should be read alongside the relevant register for the latest observations.

Where the tool and the current approved register differ, the register takes precedence for ACM identification, condition and inspection information. The tool takes precedence only for program decisions, assumptions, timing and cost forecasts.

## 3 Program Objectives

### 3.1 Program Objectives

The objectives of the program are to:

- Develop a structured method for prioritising asbestos removal.
- Apply a consistent basis for assessing removal across AAD stations.
- Align removal with planned maintenance, refurbishment, demolition and capital works.
- Identify the indicative scope, timing and delivery considerations for future removal.
- Record assumptions, constraints, dependencies and decision criteria.
- Forecast removal and reinstatement costs for long-term asset and financial planning.

### 3.2 Program Duration

AAD and WSP discussed whether all ACM could be removed from the stations within 10 years and agreed that this target is not realistic or appropriate, for the following reasons:

- An accelerated program would disrupt station operations.
- Housing and mobilising a large workforce at remote stations is difficult.
- Asbestos removal in remote regions is costly.
- A 10-year target may not be the best whole-of-life approach where demolition, replacement or major maintenance is expected after that period.
- The risk of a longer timeframe can be readily managed.

The ARP keeps a 10-year planning timeframe, which is longer than most of AAD's forecast project planning. Where removal is pushed beyond 10 years, the reason is recorded in the tool.

### 3.3 Program Development Approach

The tool is the planning basis for ACM removal across the four stations. It records each register item, the removal decision and its assumptions, and the resulting labour, freight and cost in the allocated season. AAD will develop the delivery program from this basis, taking account of factors outside the tool. These include when each building can be taken out of service, bed capacity and trade availability, freight and resupply voyages, other works on station, annual budgets, and whether station staff or contractors carry out the work. Scope and quantities should be confirmed before a season is committed, and the tool updated to match.

The tool places each item in a single season for both removal and reinstatement. This suits most items, but for a few large ones it produces a labour and cost figure in one year that is not realistic, and these appear as spikes in the program summary.

Once AAD decides to proceed with a large item and the scope is developed further, the work can be spread out. Removal could be done in one season and reinstatement in the next, or the building could be split into zones, each removed and reinstated in turn. The total does not change, but the demand on beds, trades and freight in any one season is reduced. For these items the year in the tool should be read as the start year.

The fire doors in the Mawson Sleeping and Medical Quarters are the clearest example. The tool carries the remaining 24 doors as one entry, giving a full winter of work and close to a million dollars in a single year, most of it for supplying and fitting certified replacement doors. In practice the doors would be replaced a floor at a time over two winters, or corridor by corridor so the building stays in use, roughly halving the load in any one season.

### 3.4 Removal Strategy

AAD's approach is planned, risk-informed and opportunity-driven removal. Higher-risk ACM may need prompt action, but removing all ACM is not always the most practical or risk-appropriate outcome. Where ACM is in good condition and can be safely managed under the AMP, it can stay in place until a suitable opportunity arises.

The program aims to reduce AAD's asbestos liability over time by prioritising removal that gives the greatest reduction in risk, operational burden and future maintenance. Removal is aligned with maintenance, refurbishment, upgrades and demolition wherever practicable to avoid duplicated effort and disruption and make best use of limited logistics and funding.

Priorities will change as asset condition, operational needs, project plans and register information change. The program is therefore a planning tool for regular review, not a commitment to carry out specific works in a given year.

### 3.5 Decision-making Principles

The following principles guide removal decisions and program development:

- **Protect health and safety**
  The protection of personnel, contractors, visitors and the environment comes first. Where ACM has a higher risk of disturbance or exposure, timely action should be considered.
- **Remove where it meaningfully reduces risk**
  Removal should target ACM where it clearly reduces risk, management effort, maintenance constraints or future operational impacts.
- **Coordinate with planned works**
  Where practicable, removal should be aligned with maintenance, refurbishment, upgrades or demolition to improve efficiency and avoid repeat disruption.
- **Avoid unnecessary disturbance**
  ACM should not be removed just because it is present. Continued management, encapsulation or enclosure may be the lower-risk option until a suitable opportunity arises.
- **Consider whole-of-life outcomes**
  Decisions should account for the removal itself and for reinstatement, maintenance, operational and lifecycle effects.
- **Recognise logistical and operational constraints**
  Priorities must reflect seasonal access, freight, accommodation, workforce availability and station operations.
- **Keep decisions traceable**
  Assumptions, dependencies, constraints and scheduling decisions should be recorded so the program can be understood, reviewed and updated.
- **Keep the program flexible**
  The ARP should be reviewed and updated as new information becomes available.

### 3.6 Key Assumptions

The ARP and its planning and costing tool are based on the following assumptions:

- Where a register entry is unconfirmed (presumed, not sampled or unable to view), it is treated as asbestos. Only items the register records as removed or tested negative are left out.
- Each year is split into two seasons, with winter first and summer second (Figure 1). "The season before demolition" means the preceding season in this order.
- The working week is five days and the working day eight hours, with four productive tool hours per day.
- Removal labour is based on two AAD worked examples from Casey Station, normalised using the complexity and accessibility adjusters and then pro-rated for other removal tasks.
- Reinstatement labour rates provided by North Projects represent productive tool time.
- Freight volumes include bulking allowances for removed ACM, reinstatement materials, PPE and basic removal equipment.
- Labour is costed at AAD staff rates with no allowance for contractors.

## 4 Prioritisation

### 4.1 Prioritisation Overview

The decision guidance is the main tool for helping AAD prioritise ACM removal. Each register item is assessed against what is known about its future. Where nothing is known, simple comparisons are used to identify removal opportunities. Figure 2 summarises the decision filters used to allocate a removal season.

The tool records each removal decision and the assumptions behind it, so it also serves as a register of who made each decision and why.

### 4.2 Primary Prioritisation

#### 4.2.1 High Risk Asbestos

The Asbestos Registers include a material risk assessment for each ACM. Where an item's risk would normally fall outside AAD's risk appetite and call for urgent removal, it is programmed for removal in the coming seasons. A register risk score of 1 is treated as high risk and programmed for the next available season.

#### 4.2.2 End-of-life

Where a structure containing ACM is due for demolition or replacement within the 10-year period, removal ahead of that date is included in the program. Where a known end-of-life date falls outside the 10 years, it is still recorded in the tool for future updates, and the item is assessed against the other filters to decide whether to remove it earlier or keep managing it under the AMP.

For planning, ACM in these structures is generally removed in the season before demolition, as late as practicable and typically in summer, so the building can be demolished the following season. For buildings housing operations AAD considers critical, such as plant rooms, the surgery or laboratories, removal and demolition are assumed to happen in the same season to limit downtime.

#### 4.2.3 Maintenance

Where ACM is due to be removed through planned maintenance, it is allocated to that maintenance season. For example, gaskets would be removed in the year the plant is replaced or serviced.

Where ACM would be disturbed to gain access for maintenance, such as an ACM access panel over services, removal should be considered before the maintenance is due. The decision should weigh the register risk assessment against the effort needed for full removal. For example, removing a large area of low-risk wall lining to reach a valve serviced once every 10 years may not be reasonable.

#### 4.2.4 Planned Station Upgrades and Projects

Where AAD plans upgrades that alter, extend or replace a structure containing ACM, the project scope should consider removing the ACM as part of the works. Removal is generally scheduled for the season before the upgrade starts.

Alliance-planned projects that affect ACM structures should be treated the same way, with removal scheduled at least one season before the works start.

### 4.3 Secondary Prioritisation

#### 4.3.1 Priority Assessment

The priority assessment is a useful secondary method. It follows international best practice for asbestos management, which also informs similar risk methods used in Australia. The register material risk score is combined with a priority score, and the result is used to compare ACM items.

The material score identifies higher-risk ACM, such as material more likely to release fibres if disturbed, but on its own it does not set removal priority. Priority should be confirmed with an assessment that also considers:

- Maintenance activity
- Occupant activity
- Likelihood of disturbance
- Human exposure potential

Together, the two assessments compare items on both fibre release potential and exposure to people, and give a basis for ranking removal. Where removal is not practicable, ACM can be protected or monitored under the AMP.

For the ARP, AAD carried out a revised material assessment and a new building use assessment to produce weighted scores. These replace the register's 4 (low) to 1 (high) scale with a 21% (low) to 100% (high) scale, giving finer resolution when comparing items.

Lower scores indicate poorer condition, greater degradation or friability, and more likely disturbance from the way the space is used, and these items should generally be ranked first. Where scores are close, such as a high-risk material in a remote plant room against a low-risk item in a busy space, the choice comes down to AAD's preference and the tertiary filters.

### 4.4 Tertiary Decision Filters

Where the primary and secondary filters do not point to a clear season, the following can help find the most practical opportunity:

- **Labour efficiency**
  AAD may prefer to group removal tasks by trade in one season or spread them across trades.
- **Packaging works**
  Combining work with other removal, maintenance or project activities on station reduces mobilisation and disruption.
- **Seasonal suitability**
  Internal and external work may suit different seasons.
- **Station access and accommodation**
  Access and bed capacity should be considered when allocating a season.
- **Freight availability**
  There should be enough freight capacity for equipment, reinstatement materials and returned ACM waste.

### 4.5 ACM Removal Beyond the Program

Some ACM may fall outside the 10-year program because it is lower priority, stable and manageable, or tied to renewal or major works planned later. These items stay under the AMP and Asbestos Registers, and their timing should be revisited if condition, access, operations or planned works change.

The following are generally excluded from the ARP or deferred beyond 10 years and managed under the AMP:

- **Heritage structures**
  Heritage-listed structures, and structures where removal would affect structural integrity, are excluded.
- **Huts**
  AAD has advised that field huts are excluded because their remoteness makes safe removal and logistics difficult. Some huts are also heritage listed.
- **Gaskets**
  Gaskets stay outside the program until replacement timing is confirmed through AAD's maintenance program, which avoids disrupting live services. Their effect on program cost is small.
- **Wilkes Station**
  Wilkes, an abandoned site near Casey, is excluded and will be dealt with in a separate future program.
- **Floor sheeting**
  Floor sheets usually cannot be fully removed without demolishing the structure. Where flooring is in good condition and unlikely to be disturbed, it can stay in place under the AMP. Partial removal during refurbishment may suit where inaccessible material, including under floor plates, can safely remain.
- **External cladding**
  Removing external cladding would need extensive dismantling and reinstatement, risks damaging the surrounding fabric or structure, and would leave the building exposed to the weather during the work. It remains under the AMP until it can be done with major refurbishment, recladding or demolition.
- **Vehicles, mobile plant and equipment**
  The ARP covers the fixed infrastructure recorded in the Asbestos Registers, including buildings, field huts, heritage sites and tip sites. Vehicles, mobile plant, tools, laboratory equipment and similar items are not included.

## 5 Removal and Reinstatement

### 5.1 Removal Scope

The removal scope for each item reflects how the material is used, how much of it can reasonably be reached, and whether removal is tied to maintenance, refurbishment, demolition or another project. Gaskets, hatches, access panels and other plant or services components are generally removed when the associated asset is maintained or replaced. Where that timing is not confirmed, they stay under the AMP rather than being given an arbitrary season.

For linings, flooring, cladding, insulation, debris and other widespread materials, the treatment also considers whether the full extent can be removed without major impact on operations, structure, services or adjoining construction. The item, material type, location, quantity and access are used to define and cost the task without prescribing the removal method. Table 1 sets out the treatment types.

### 5.2 Reinstatement Scope

Reinstatement depends on whether the removed item performs an ongoing function, forms part of the building fabric or is needed for an asset to operate. Linings, flooring, gaskets, insulation and similar components are assumed to be replaced with a modern asbestos-free equivalent. The quantity matches the area removed, with a bulking allowance for freight to station. Where modern products are thicker than the material removed, the thinnest commercially available product is used to estimate volume and freight.

Reinstatement is generally not needed where ACM is removed for demolition or full replacement of a structure. Removal timing for these items follows Section 4.2.2.

### 5.3 Completed Entries

Once an item has been fully removed, it no longer needs to appear as a future task. As the ARP is a planning tool, completed entries should preferably be deleted. Where only part of an item has been removed, such as some flooring, the entry should stay and be updated to show what remains. The tool allows for the following:

- **Delete the entry**
  The row can be deleted, provided the matching entry is also removed from the Asbestos Register so the two stay aligned.
- **Retain the entry as Complete**
  Clear the Requirement for Removal, Season and Est. Year for Works fields, set the Treatment Type to Complete, and note in the Removal Commentary that the item has been addressed (Figure 3).
- **Retain the entry as Manage per AMP if partially removed**
  Set the Treatment Type to Manage per AMP, clear the Requirement for Removal, Season and Est. Year for Works fields, and note in the Removal Commentary what has been removed and what remains.

## 6 Cost Management

### 6.1 Estimation Methodology

The cost model is:

#### 6.1.1 Removal Labour Estimation

ACM removal rates are hard to estimate in Antarctica, where productivity is affected by short work windows, remote logistics, containment and waste handling, and the need to work around station operations.

With little comparable data available, and bottom-up estimates too time-consuming and detailed for a 10-year program, a baseline was developed from two AAD removal projects using the recorded ACM volume and labour time: the Red Shed duct board removal (2025/26) and the Old Trades (Chippies) Workshop (2018/19). The two examples were normalised using the cost adjusters, and the resulting rate is applied pro rata to the estimated ACM volume of every other task.

#### 6.1.2 Freight

##### 6.1.2.1 To Australia Freight

Return freight is based on the estimated volume of ACM removed plus the contaminated PPE and equipment used. Quantities were estimated for each register item by measuring the affected area from AAD drawings, station models and photographs, with thicknesses and item counts assumed where they could not be confirmed. The measurements and assumptions are recorded in the tool's assumptions column, and items with no basis for a quantity are carried as Manage per AMP until measured. A bulking factor is applied to allow for packaging, padding, inefficient packing and the natural bulking of the material.

##### 6.1.2.2 From Australia Freight

Outbound freight covers PPE and equipment, scaled to the ACM volume using the equipment list from the Old Trades Workshop project.

Where reinstatement is needed, the material volume is taken as equal to the ACM removed before bulking, with a lower bulking factor to reflect more efficient packing of new materials. Some reinstatement options use a minimum product thickness greater than the material removed, which increases the freight volume.

##### 6.1.2.3 Freight Rates

AAD provided freight rates, with the same rate used to and from Australia. The 2025/26 season rates are shown in Table 2.

#### 6.1.3 Labour

Removal labour days from Section 6.1.1 are multiplied by the AAD hourly rates in Table 3 to give the labour cost of each task. The rates are for the 2025/26 season, and the labour and G&S components are added to give the total rate.

Reinstatement labour rates were provided by North Projects as hours per square metre.

#### 6.1.4 Cost Adjusters

So that labour cost does not scale purely in proportion to volume, removal labour is increased by adjusters for complexity and accessibility. The two adjusters are added together and applied to the labour for each task.

##### 6.1.4.1 Complexity Adjuster

The complexity adjuster reflects how involved the removal is, based on the item, the scale of the work and the dismantling or interfaces involved. It does not account for condition, friability or access. A discrete item such as a gasket is simpler than widespread flooring or wall lining, while foundations and other large or involved work attract a higher adjustment. The adjusters are given in Table 4.

##### 6.1.4.2 Accessibility Adjuster

The accessibility adjuster covers factors that slow removal, such as restricted access, work in occupied or operational areas, the need for access equipment or isolation, and the condition and friability of the ACM. The register material risk rating is part of this: ratings of 3 to 4 generally indicate easy removal, 2 more challenging removal, and 1 extreme conditions. The adjuster is chosen on the overall circumstances of the task. The adjusters are given in Table 5.

#### 6.1.5 Inflation Adjuster

Future costs are adjusted for inflation using a default rate of 3.5% a year, which can be changed in the tool.

In the equation, the rate r is 0.035 and n is the number of years from the current year.

#### 6.1.6 Reinstatement Material Cost

North Projects provided reinstatement material rates per unit, covering items from individual gaskets to large areas of wall lining. The quantities are based on the unfactored ACM removal quantities.

#### 6.1.7 Exclusions

The cost estimate excludes the following:

- Disposal of the returned ACM in Australia.
- Wooden crates sent to station flat-packed for returning ACM, which are difficult to cost.
- AAD planning and processing time. Labour covers on-site time only.
- Any savings from more efficient shipping or logistics.

## 7 Constraints

The ARP is a high-level planning tool, and its outputs are subject to the following constraints:

- **Station resourcing and competing priorities**
  Removal depends on the availability of trained personnel and trades at each station, which must be shared with maintenance, capital works and other station activities. The tool costs labour at AAD staff rates. Using contractors would add beds, travel and external rates but reduce the demand on station trades. Large removals, such as whole-building demolitions, will most likely need professional removalists, and AAD will need to decide this job by job.
- **Accommodation and bed capacity**
  Bed capacity limits how many extra people can be on station for removal and reinstatement. The tool gives a high-level indication of bed demand, but actual allocation depends on seasonal population planning.
- **Logistics, freight and waste return**
  Removal relies on enough freight capacity for personnel, equipment, PPE, containment and replacement materials, and for returning packaged ACM waste. Freight or storage limits may mean work is deferred, split across seasons or combined with other works.
- **Seasonal access and environmental conditions**
  Access windows, transport schedules, weather, temperature and daylight all limit delivery, particularly for external work, work at height and work outside the heated envelope.
- **Station operations and project interfaces**
  Removal may require occupied areas, critical services, plant or access routes to be isolated, affecting station operations and science. Work therefore needs to be coordinated with maintenance shutdowns, renewals, demolition, upgrades and other projects.
- **Removal complexity, access and reinstatement**
  Concealed material, restricted spaces, work at height, nearby services and ACM built into the structure can slow removal or prevent complete removal. Enabling works and reinstatement may also drive the timing, cost and extent of removal.
- **Source information and uncertainty**
  Program outputs rely on the accuracy and currency of the registers, quantity estimates, condition information, maintenance plans and end-of-life dates. Changes or gaps in these inputs may change priorities, timing, labour, freight and cost.

## 8 10 Year Removal Program Outcomes

These findings are based on the version 7 Asbestos Registers for Casey, Davis, Macquarie Island and Mawson, together with AAD advice up to 11 November 2026. The station programs apply the prioritisation approach in Section 4 and the assumptions in Section 3.6, and the full breakdown is in Appendix C. Sections 8.1 to 8.4 set out, for each station, the number of buildings that become ACM free and the main findings.

The program does not include items managed per AMP, such as gaskets, whose removal depends on future maintenance and cannot be predicted. Items without a quantity, and items in huts and heritage buildings, are also not costed, so the actual volume of ACM removed is expected to be higher.

### 8.1 Casey

Figure 4 summarises the 10-year program for Casey Station. Four buildings can be made completely ACM free within the 10 years: the ANARESAT Equipment Building, Balloon Release Building, Receiver Building and Red Shed. The Transmitter Building reaches end of life within the program, and its ACM is removed ahead of demolition in 2037. The Main Power House and Operations Building cannot be made ACM free without full demolition because of their floor sheets and subfloor.

Key findings:

- Spending peaks at $264k in 2032, driven by removal of the Main Power House dado wall lining.
- ACM at Jack's Donga (insulation), Mabel Hut and Robinson's Ridge Hut (floors) and around the old Carpenter's Workshop (debris) has no quantity and is not in the program.
- The six Wilkes entries are excluded at AAD's request.
- Five fuel tank gasket entries are managed per AMP and will be replaced during tank maintenance.
- The Red Shed crawlway is costed at $149k in 2029, but the register notes the main subfloor sheets were removed in 2025. AAD should review the quantity.

### 8.2 Davis

Figure 5 summarises the 10-year program for Davis Station. Two items can be made completely ACM free within the 10 years: the Sleeping and Medical Quarters plant room and the Site Services J1 stair base. The Emergency Power House (2028) and Main Power House (2032) reach end of life within the program, and their ACM is removed ahead of or during demolition. The Green Store and the Site Services footings cannot be made ACM free without full demolition because of the mezzanine floor sheets and formwork cast into the foundations.

Key findings:

- Spending peaks at $734k in 2030, mainly for the General Workshop internal wall sheets.
- A smaller peak of $271k in 2028 covers the Emergency Power House demolition items and the friable plant room coating in the Sleeping and Medical Quarters.
- The Old Tip Site (buried) and loose pieces around Watts Hut have no quantity and are not in the program.
- The General Workshop trades office vinyl tiles are now covered with Regupol and are managed per AMP.
- Brookes Hut and Watts Hut are managed per AMP as huts.
- Seven gasket entries are managed per AMP, and the Main Power House gaskets will go with the building at demolition.
- The Old Garage Site and beach slab are recorded as Complete.

### 8.3 Mawson

Figure 6 summarises the 10-year program for Mawson Station. No retained building can be made completely ACM free within the 10 years. The Transmitter Building reaches end of life within the program, and its ACM is removed before and during demolition in 2036 and 2037. Aeronomy, the ANARESAT Equipment Building, Green Store, Main Power House and Sleeping and Medical Quarters cannot be made ACM free without full demolition because of floor and underfloor sheeting, and the Tankhouse and Water Supply Building also retain formwork cast into their foundations.

Key findings:

- Spending peaks at $937k in 2032, almost entirely for the Sleeping and Medical Quarters fire doors. This could be staged over two seasons (Section 3.3).
- Ten heritage buildings are managed per AMP and excluded from removal: Biscoe, Cosray, Dog Hut, Dovers, Electricians Workshop, Hangar, PID Van, Science Building (Wombat), Shackleton and Wilkins.
- ACM in the Dovers partitions, East Arm tip, Shackleton walls and switchboard, Main Power House electrical box, and the tiled areas and stair landing formwork in the Sleeping and Medical Quarters has no quantity and is not in the program.
- Removal of the Incinerator Building switchboard depends on the building's end-of-life date, which is not yet confirmed.
- Ten gasket entries are managed per AMP. The Main Power House generator gaskets and Water Supply pump gaskets are recorded as Complete.

### 8.4 Macquarie Island

Figure 7 summarises the 10-year program for Macquarie Island Station. No retained building can be made completely ACM free within the 10 years. The Emergency Radio Hut, Emergency Power House and Electrical Distribution Hut reach end of life in 2035, and their ACM is removed ahead of or during decommissioning. The Transmitter Hut, Proton Processing Hut and Magnetic Variometer Building were demolished in the 2026 season. Internal linings, ceilings and soffits are removed from the main station buildings over the program, but most of these buildings keep cement sheet behind external timber cladding or under floor coverings that can only come out with demolition.

Key findings:

- Spending peaks at $1.12M in 2034, driven by the two Biology Laboratory ceiling liners ($932k combined, 164 labour days). These assume both an internal lining and a roof space lining, which AAD should confirm.
- ACM at the Meteorology Office and Meteorology Technician's Workshop (all items), Pump House brewery linings, Sauna cold porch, SAD bathroom walls, Mechanical Workshop external sheeting and ceiling space debris, Green Store debris, Flammable Liquid Hut soffit, tip site, loading ramp pipe and fire hydrant pipe has no quantity and is not in the program.
- Bauer Bay Hut, Fire Hut, Green Gorge Hut, Flammable Liquid Hut and the seal hide are managed per AMP as huts.
- End-of-life dates were not available for most buildings, so removal years for Preference items are a planning assumption spread across the program.
